# Competitive Landscape: Focus Bear vs. Competitors

## 1. Research Overview
* **Freedom & Cold Turkey:** These are strict blockers. They excel at preventing access to distracting websites and apps. However, they are "negative" constraints—they stop bad behavior but don't actively guide you toward positive habits.
* **Tiimo & Routinery:** These are visual planners and habit trackers, highly favored by the neurodivergent community. They are great at visualizing routines but lack the strict enforcement (the "teeth") to stop you from ignoring their notifications and scrolling social media instead.

## 2. Reflection Questions

### What makes Focus Bear different from these apps?
Focus Bear bridges the gap between passive habit tracking and aggressive blocking. It is "habit stacking with teeth." It doesn't just block distractions; it physically locks the screen to enforce a morning routine (like stretching or journaling) before unlocking the computer for the day. 

### If you were a user, why would you choose Focus Bear over competitors?
I would choose Focus Bear for its "forced initiation." When struggling with executive dysfunction, swiping away a Tiimo notification is too easy. Freedom only blocks the bad stuff without guiding the good. Focus Bear's full-screen takeover completely removes the need for willpower to start a positive routine.

### What’s one feature that other apps have that Focus Bear doesn’t?
**Beautiful, intuitive visual timelines.** Apps like Tiimo feature highly polished, drag-and-drop visual schedules that make time feel tangible. Focus Bear's interface is more utilitarian and list-focused, which can be slightly less engaging for visual thinkers.

### Based on your research, what’s one improvement you think Focus Bear could make?
**Streamlined Onboarding and UI Polish.** Competitors like Routinery and Freedom have incredibly smooth, low-friction setups. Focus Bear's initial setup can feel slightly overwhelming due to the sheer number of permissions required and the rigid configuration process. Simplifying the initial user journey would greatly improve adoption.
